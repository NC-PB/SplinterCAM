# 4. Pocketing: the closed pocket of plan 0008

This file holds the part of topic 04 that plan 0008 needs: one closed pocket from a DXF (an outer loop and islands), cleared by contour-parallel loops from the inside out, climb only, entered by a helix, in equal depth passes. Raster and zigzag clearing, spirals, rest machining, open pockets, corner fillets, feed classes, several pockets per operation and finishing passes stay in Project Spike's topic 04 until a plan pulls them (D-158).

**Provenance.** Classes used: L (Held 1991 book SRC-030 and paper SRC-025, Veeramani and Gau 2000 SRC-042, Choy and Chan SRC-134 to SRC-136, Iliopoulos and Vosniakos 2024 in SRC-034) and O (NCXchange SRC-112, Clipper2 SRC-122 through offset2d); decisions (D-nnn); own derivation marked "(ours)".

## Notation

B: the pocket boundary, all loops at once (outer loop and islands). R: tool radius. e: side allowance (research 22 writes s for it). e_f: floor allowance. s: stepover, the distance between neighbouring levels. t_k: the clearance of level k. O_k: the region of tool centres at level k. a and u: offset2d's ArcTolerance and grid unit (a = max(0.05·tol, 2u), u = 10⁻⁴ mm; D-132). t_flat: the flattening tolerance (offset2d SPEC). Lengths in mm, angles in rad inside the code (D-028), degrees only in declarations.

## Scope

- In: one closed pocket region read from a DXF: exactly one outer loop and zero or more islands inside it, no island inside another, every edge a material wall; a box stock that contains the region in XY; one flat end mill of radius R; side allowance e and floor allowance e_f (D-150); z_top, z_bottom, the largest step-down a_p,max, spindle speed, cutting and plunge feed entered by hand (D-025); stepover s ≤ R; contour-parallel levels with the coverage check; a component tree cut from the inside out, climb only (D-032); a helix entry in every leaf (D-079); links through the clearance height between chains and depth levels (D-062, D-087); equal depth passes as in research 22.
- Out (Later, one line each, no work):
  - stepover above R with D-094's local reduction and the extra centre level; the actual (worn) radius in the coverage check (Q-076 answer);
  - slot-through links between branches of the tree (D-045) and the slot feed class (D-089); feed classes per span;
  - corner fillets on clearing loops (D-046) and their cap (D-109);
  - ramp entry, entry at pre-drilled points, a straight plunge, the tool's RMPX as the angle limit (D-079, Q-120);
  - finishing passes on the wall and the floor (they need leads, research 10 in Project Spike); spring passes;
  - open pockets and air edges (D-089: open pockets start from air), several pockets in one operation and their order;
  - raster, one-way and zigzag clearing (D-025, D-088, D-082), spirals (D-129), rest machining (D-083);
  - conventional milling and outside-in order (D-032 makes them template options later);
  - arc fitting of the loops (D-023), the stock update for later operations (D-026);
  - a better entry point than the centroid (the medial axis comes in release 2, D-035);
  - the centre-line pass for a slot exactly 2(R + e) wide (Held note, item 15, in Project Spike's topic 04).

## Method

1. **Region** (ours). The loops come from io as curve rows, one outer loop and its islands. geometry2d's loop tree normalises them: the outer loop counter-clockwise, islands clockwise. Every source ID gets the class MATERIAL (D-059). Anything else stops the operation with `POCKET_REGION` (error, ours): no outer loop, two outer loops, an island outside the outer loop or inside another island. The region's bounding box must lie inside the stock box's XY extent, or `POCKET_STOCK_SMALL` (error, ours, as REQ-PRF-009).
2. **Levels.** t_1 = R + e and t_{k+1} = t_k + s. Each level is O_k = `offset_region(B, AIR, t_k)`, offset from the source loops by the total distance t_k, never from O_{k−1} (D-132; offset2d REQ-OFF-033). Levels are made until offset2d returns `OFFSET_EMPTY` (REQ-OFF-039). If O_1 is already empty, the tool does not fit, a slot exactly 2(R + e) wide included: `POCKET_TOOL_TOO_LARGE` (error, ours; SRC-030, Held note, item 15, gives the exact-fit case).
3. **Components and tree.** offset2d returns loops without nesting (REQ-OFF-037): a counter-clockwise loop is an outer boundary, a clockwise loop a hole. The strategy nests each hole into the outer loop that contains a probe of it (a vertex that is not a fixed node, or an edge midpoint where all are fixed), by geometry2d's `point_in_region` (research 02, Pinch points and nesting). A component is one outer loop with its holes. Each component of O_{k+1} lies in exactly one component of O_k, because O_{k+1} ⊆ O_k, so the components form a tree, a forest when O_1 has several components (SRC-030, Lemmas 7.5 and 7.7; Held note, item 2). The parent is found by the same probe test. A node with several children marks a strait whose half-width lies between t_k and t_{k+1}; a hole that vanishes marks a strait between an island and another wall (same item). When O_1 has more than one component, the strait between them is narrower than 2(R + e) and its material stays: `POCKET_SPLIT` (warning, ours) names the number of parts.
4. **Stepover and coverage.** With s ≤ R contour-parallel levels leave no material: a point whose clearance lies in [t_k, t_k + R] is within R of the loop at t_k, and the last level leaves no core because O_{n+1} is empty, so every clearance is below t_n + s ≤ t_n + R (SRC-030, p. 25: "a pass distance of one radius never leaves material"; Held note, item 3, proof from Def. 8.4, Cor. 8.1 and Lemma 5.10). Above R the corner rule s ≤ R(1 + sin(β/2)) for a line corner of interior angle β is only an estimate (SRC-030, pp. 26–27, Czeranowsky; SRC-042, Eq. 1): inward offsets create new sharp tips (SRC-042 note, limit 1) and leave a core. So plan 0008 allows s ≤ R only, and D-094's local reduction above R comes later. The check still runs, as a guard against faults in the levels (ours): the material the levels must remove at one depth is everything a tool with its centre in O_1 can reach, dilate(O_1, R). That equals the band swept by the outermost loop together with erode(O_1, R) = offset(B, −(2R + e)) (ours: a point of dilate(O_1, R) is either within R of the boundary of O_1 or at least R inside it). The outermost loop is one of the passes, so the rest is rest = `offset_region(B, AIR, 2R + e)` minus `machined_area(every loop of every level, R + μ)`, by offset2d's `boolean` DIFFERENCE. μ = 2·t_flat + 9u + a makes up for machined_area's sweep, which lies between R − (2·t_flat + 9u + a) and R − 3u from the path (offset2d REQ-OFF-032), so the computed sweep holds the true one (ours). A rest that is not empty is `POCKET_UNCUT` (error, ours). It is checked once per operation, since every depth level has the same loops. Both forms of the rest were compared numerically (Tests 3 to 5).
5. **Entry point of a leaf** (ours, with SRC-030's fit test). Every leaf of the tree gets one entry. Its point c is the centroid of the leaf's region if `point_in_region` puts it inside the leaf; for a leaf with a hole whose centroid lies in the hole, c is the midpoint of the shortest segment between the leaf's outer loop and its first hole; otherwise the operation stops with `POCKET_NO_ENTRY` (error) naming the leaf's position. d_c is the exact distance from c to B, the smallest of geometry2d's `closest_point` over all rows (lines and arcs, not the flattened polygon). The helix radius is r_h = min(0.9·R, d_c − R − e) (D-079: the largest radius from 0.9·R down that fits). A helix of radius r_h about c fits iff c lies in offset(B, −(R + e + r_h)), that is d_c ≥ R + e + r_h (SRC-030, Held note, item 11; A-005 result). If r_h < 0.2·R, no helix fits by D-079: `POCKET_NO_ENTRY` (error) names the leaf, its d_c and the radius the tool would need.
6. **Seam** (SRC-030, Lemma 5.10 and pp. 122–125; Held note, item 7). q is the point of B nearest to c (from the same `closest_point` calls; on a tie within eps_len the first row in the region's order, ours) and u = (q − c)/d_c. Along the segment c → q the clearance falls linearly from d_c to 0, so every level the chain passes crosses it once, at the point x_k at distance d_c − t_k from c, on a loop of the component that holds c. Because offset2d's loops lie in the band [t_k, t_k + a + 6u], x_k is taken as the exact intersection of the segment with that component's loops (orient2d), the one nearest c on a multiple hit (ours), and inserted as a vertex. Each loop of the chain starts and ends at its x_k. A step-out x_{k+1} → x_k is s long, keeps clearance at least t_k and cannot gouge; it is a full-width cut (SRC-030, pp. 31 and 34).
7. **Chains, inside out** (D-032; SRC-030, p. 28: compute the offsets inward, build the path outward from the innermost point). The tree is walked in post-order; siblings come in offset2d's canonical order (REQ-OFF-038), the forest's roots too. Every leaf starts a chain. A chain climbs from its leaf to the parent only from the parent's last child; the chain of an earlier child ends at that child's outermost loop, and the tool retracts (ours: D-045's retract branch until the slot feed of D-089 exists; plan 0008, question 3). So every loop is cut after all loops inside it, and the number of entries per depth level equals the number of leaves.
8. **Loops of one component at one level** (ours, after SRC-030, pp. 22–24, Held's island bridges; Held note, item 10). A component with holes has several loops. They are joined by a minimum spanning tree over the loops, each edge weighted by the shortest distance between the two loops (closest points of the two polygons, compared as squared lengths), ties by loop order (ours). The shortest segment between two boundary loops of one component lies in that component: if it met a third loop, the two shorter pieces would both be lighter edges and the tree would not take it (ours). Each edge is still checked: it must cross no loop edge of the level except at its ends (exact orient2d), or the operation stops with `POCKET_LINK_BLOCKED` (error, a guard, ours). The root is the loop that holds the chain's x_k. The tool runs the root loop from x_k; at each edge's foot, in order along the loop, it goes along the edge to the other loop, runs that loop round from the edge's foot (with its own edges, recursively), returns along the edge and goes on. Every tree edge is cut twice. Loops touching at a fixed node give an edge of length 0: the tool passes the node, no move.
9. **Direction.** offset2d returns outer loops counter-clockwise and holes clockwise (REQ-OFF-037). Run so, the region lies on the left of the feed and the uncut band outside the loop, toward the walls and the islands, on the right: climb milling with the spindle clockwise (SRC-030, Held note, item 9; D-032; research 22, Method 2 for the same rule on a profile). No loop is reversed.
10. **Depth levels** as research 22, Method 3: n = ⌈(z_top − z_b) / a_p,max⌉ levels of equal depth Δ = (z_top − z_b)/n at z_i = z_top − i·Δ, with z_b = z_bottom + e_f (D-150, ours to apply it to the bottom only), n reduced by one where (n − 1)·a_p,max ≥ z_top − z_b − eps_len (REQ-PRF-016's rule). The depth checks are the profile's, with the depth measured from the stock top (REQ-PRF-004 to 008 as `POCKET_` codes). The levels are cut one after another: all chains at z_1, then all at z_2 (ours: the plane plan is the same at every level). Every level enters each leaf at the same point, so from z_2 on each helix starts in the area the level above has already cleared: entering a region from already machined area needs no predrilled hole (SRC-025, pp. 303 and 306).
11. **Helix** (D-079, D-096; research 26, Arcs and helices). At z_i the helix of a leaf starts at S_h = c + r_h·u and at z_s = min(h_r, z_prev + g), with z_prev the stock top for i = 1 and z_{i−1} otherwise, and g = air gap ratio · R, D-079's air gap 0.2·R applied along the axis (ours). The largest pitch is p_max = 2π·r_h·tan α, with α the declared helix angle, tan α from geometry2d's kernel `basic_sin_cos` (REQ-G2D-240), not NumPy (D-055). The helix has N = ⌈(z_s − z_i)/p_max⌉ whole turns (ours: whole turns end at S_h, so no end point needs a sine), N reduced by one where (N − 1)·p_max ≥ z_s − z_i − eps_len. Each turn is one ARC about c with sweep +2π (counter-clockwise: material outside the bore on the right, climb), its end at S_h in XY and p = (z_s − z_i)/N lower (ours: one record per turn keeps |sweep| ≤ 2π). The real angle atan(p/(2π·r_h)) is at most α. One flat turn at z_i follows (sweep +2π, travel 0), which removes the ramp the helix leaves on the floor (ours). Then a LINE from S_h to the leaf loop's x_n along the seam.
12. **Moves of one chain at z_i**, in order: RAPID to S_h at h_c; RAPID down to h_r; LINE down to z_s at the plunge feed (role APPROACH; left out when z_s = h_r); the N helix turns at the plunge feed (role PLUNGE); the flat turn at the plunge feed (role CUT); LINE S_h → x_n at the cutting feed (CUT); then per level of the chain the loops of method 8 from x_k round to x_k at the cutting feed (CUT), and the step-out x_k → x_{k−1} (CUT) before the next; at the chain's end RAPID up along Z to h_c. The operation starts with RAPID along Z to h_c after the tool change and ends at h_c (research 10, Method 1 and 4). Every RAPID in the plane is at h_c; every move below h_r is a feed move (D-062, D-087; G0 along the axis only). Feed classes: `cut` and `plunge`, as the profile's (REQ-PRF-018).

## Traps

1. **Offsetting an offset rounds twice** and walks the levels toward the wall: every level comes from the source loops (D-132; research 18, pitfall 2.3).
2. **Nesting by a fixed node** gives the wrong parent where loops touch: probe a vertex that is not fixed (research 02, Pinch points and nesting; pitfall 2.8).
3. **Critical distances.** Where a level's t_k equals a strait's half-width or a lobe's largest clearance, the tree changes with the smallest input change; offset2d takes the integer result as it is (REQ-OFF-026). Golden cases keep every level at least 1 mm from such a distance.
4. **Corners and step-outs cut at full width.** At a sharp corner of the inner loop the engagement rises by about the corner's turn: at a 90° corner, about 150° at s = 0.5·R (60° on the straight) and about 180° at s = R (90° on the straight) (our simulation, ±1°; SRC-134 note, test 3: 179 to 180° at s = R = 5). Step-outs, the move out of the bore and the tree edges are full-width cuts (SRC-030, pp. 31 and 34). Plan 0008 has one cutting feed, so it must suit a full-width cut; the setup sheet says so. Feed classes come with D-089.
5. **Determinism** (D-055): the helix's tan α and every count (levels, turns, depth levels) come from comparisons, sums, products, sqrt and the kernel's sin and cos, never NumPy trigonometry; a quotient one ulp over an integer gives no extra turn or level.
6. **The centroid is not the deepest point.** It can refuse a leaf where a helix at a better point would fit, and a crescent leaf can have its centroid outside: the refusal names the leaf; better entry points come with the medial axis (D-035) or a ramp.
7. **Material left in a strait.** When O_1 splits, the strait between its parts is narrower than 2(R + e) and keeps its material (`POCKET_SPLIT`, warning); the corners of the walls keep the radius R as always.
8. **Wall marks.** With e = 0 the outermost loop cuts the wall at every depth level, so the wall shows a step at each level; a finishing pass with a lead-in comes later.
9. **Helix and the tool's centre.** The helix keeps the axis moving on a circle of at least 0.2·R; nothing plunges straight into material. Whether a tool cuts at its centre is not in the tool data of release 1 (Q-120).
10. **Feed on a helix** is measured along the 3D path (D-096); a control that takes it in the plane runs faster by 1/cos α: 1.0006 at 2°, 1.0038 at 5°, below any feed tolerance.
11. **Patent rules** (D-130, D-143): the stepover is constant, there is no lower engagement limit, the island loops are levels of the offset, not paths added because the tool met the island, and no region is split by comparing times.
